Voltage-sensor gating: the paddle model

Redrawn from the description in Long, Tao, Campbell & MacKinnon, Nature 2007;450:376–382 (Fig. 6 and its legend; Discussion). Schematic, not to atomic scale.

0 mV · depolarized OPEN · from the crystal structure

The crystal structure is the open state at 0 mV. The closed state is the authors' model, in which the S1–S2 half holds position while the S3b–S4 paddle slides about 15 Å toward the intracellular side.

What the animation shows

Every element below is taken from the paper's own description, not from a later textbook version.

Legend of the drawing

  • S1, S2, S3a — held in place by the S1–pore contact
  • S3b–S4 — the paddle that moves
  • R1–R4, K5, R6 — gating charges on S4
  • Two negative clusters, ≈15 Å apart
  • F233 — the phenylalanine gap
  • S4–S5 linker, resting on S6
  • S6 inner helices — they converge to close the gate

The paper's own words

“The S1 and S2 helices are hypothesized to maintain their position, whereas the S3–S4 paddle has moved inward. The positive charges on S4 now reach towards the intracellular solution, and are stabilized through interactions with the internal negative cluster.”
— Fig. 6b legend
“The α-carbon position of R1 is adjacent to the phenylalanine, representing a displacement perpendicular to the plane of the membrane of approximately 15 Å relative to its location in the open structure.”
— Fig. 6b legend
“The inward displacement of the S4 helix pushes down on the N-terminal end of the S4–S5 linker helix, causing it to tilt towards the intracellular side and to close the pore.”
— Fig. 6b legend
“Because of the shape of the voltage sensor and its effect on membrane thickness, 15 Å would be sufficient to transfer the charges from the electrical outside to the electrical inside.”
— Discussion
“The arginine side chains must apparently flip between charge clusters on their way across the membrane… the absence of polar groups in the central zone might prevent proton transfer across the membrane… and it might also create an energy barrier for the arginines, which would favour a switch-like transition.”
— Discussion, the “phenylalanine gap”
“The zone of 3₁₀-helical secondary structure shifts as a moving segment along S4 in a wave-like fashion. Such a ‘concertina effect’ would turn arginine residues away from the lipid membrane and towards the inside of the voltage sensor as they cross the centre of the hydrophobic core without necessitating rigid body rotation of the S4 helix.”
— Discussion

Scale

8 px = 1 Å. The hydrophobic core is drawn as 240 px = 30 Å; the paddle travel is 120 px = 15 Å, about half the core.

Two caveats worth saying out loud

  • The closed state was never solved. Panels b–d of Fig. 6 are models, and the authors say so.
  • The 15 Å number comes from functional experiments (MTS accessibility, histidine scanning, biotin–avidin accessibility) mapped onto the structure, not from the crystal itself.
Source: Long SB, Tao X, Campbell EB, MacKinnon R. Atomic structure of a voltage-dependent K⁺ channel in a lipid membrane-like environment. Nature 2007;450:376–382. Quotes are reproduced from the paper's Fig. 6 legend and Discussion.
