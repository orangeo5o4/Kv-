Voltage-sensor gating in a Kv channel — 3D

Idealised model built from the description in Long, Tao, Campbell & MacKinnon, Nature 2007;450:376–382 (Figs. 3, 4 and 6 and their legends).

1 grid unit = 1 Å · membrane hydrophobic core 30 Å · paddle travel 15 Å

What this model shows

Four subunits around one central pore. Each subunit has a voltage-sensor domain on the outside and contributes two helices to the pore in the middle. When the membrane depolarizes, the S3b–S4 paddle of each sensor slides about 15 Å toward the intracellular side, the S4–S5 linker tilts, the four S6 inner helices converge, and K⁺ conduction stops.

Legend

  • S1, S2, S3a — the half that holds position
  • S3b, S4 — the paddle that moves
  • S5, S6 and the pore helix
  • R1–R4, K5, R6 — gating charges on S4
  • E183, E226 (external) and E154, E236, D259 (internal) — negative clusters ≈15 Å apart
  • F233 — the phenylalanine at the membrane midpoint
  • S4–S5 linker
  • K⁺ ions
  • lipid head groups

Three things to point at while it runs

  • Relative position. Switch to the top view: the four voltage sensors sit on the outside and the pore sits in the middle. Sensing and conduction are physically separate modules.
  • What moves and what does not. S1, S2 and S3a stay put; only the S3b–S4 paddle travels. The 15 Å guide measures that travel on R1.
  • Consequence at the gate. The S4–S5 linker is pushed down, the S6 helices converge, and the K⁺ stream stops.

The paper's own words

“The S1 and S2 helices are hypothesized to maintain their position, whereas the S3–S4 paddle has moved inward. The positive charges on S4 now reach towards the intracellular solution, and are stabilized through interactions with the internal negative cluster.”
— Fig. 6b legend
“The α-carbon position of R1 is adjacent to the phenylalanine, representing a displacement perpendicular to the plane of the membrane of approximately 15 Å relative to its location in the open structure.”
— Fig. 6b legend
“The inward displacement of the S4 helix pushes down on the N-terminal end of the S4–S5 linker helix, causing it to tilt towards the intracellular side and to close the pore.”
— Fig. 6b legend
“The voltage-sensor paddle is tilted away from the central axis of the voltage sensor, towards the lipid membrane … One entire face of the voltage-sensor paddle is exposed to the lipid membrane.”
— Results, and Fig. 1c legend
“Phenylalanine 233, located near the midpoint of the membrane, separates the external and internal negative clusters … This zone contains a phenylalanine residue … in 355 out of 360 Kv channels, and therefore we call it the ‘phenylalanine gap’.”
— Results and Discussion
“The selectivity filter leads to a water-filled cavity that contains another K ion, near the middle of the membrane, and then the pore narrows again until it becomes closed to ion flow by converging helices near the intracellular surface.”
— this is the pore architecture as summarised in Gadsby 2009 for KcsA; the same architecture is present in the paddle-chimaera.

Honest caveats

  • This is an idealised model, not the deposited coordinates. Helices are drawn as smooth α-helices at the radii and lengths reported for the paddle-chimaera; the four-fold arrangement, the two negative clusters, F233, the ~10 Å hydrophobic central zone and the 15 Å travel all follow the paper.
  • The closed state was never solved. The closing motion here is the paper's Fig. 6b–d hypothesis, animated.
  • The 15 Å figure comes from functional experiments (MTS accessibility, histidine scanning, biotin–avidin accessibility) mapped onto the structure, not from the crystal itself.
  • Ion throughput (~10⁸ K⁺ s⁻¹) is far too fast to animate literally; the ion stream is slowed by many orders of magnitude.

One more version of the same idea

The paper depicts the paddle as a rigid unit that slides. It also offers an alternative in which an alpha-to-3-10 helical transition sweeps along S4 like a wave, the “concertina effect”, turning arginines away from the lipid without rigid-body rotation. This animation shows the rigid-slide version; the other version is drawn in the 2D animation shipped next to this file.
